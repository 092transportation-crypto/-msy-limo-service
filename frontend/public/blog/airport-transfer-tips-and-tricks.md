# Airport Transfer Tips & Tricks for MSY: What Frequent Flyers Know

Frequent MSY travelers develop small habits that make a real difference on transfer day — not secrets, exactly, just details that don't occur to someone flying through Louis Armstrong New Orleans International for the first time. Here's what they know.

## Always Provide Your Flight Number, Never Just an Arrival Time

This is the single most useful thing you can do for your own pickup. An arrival time is a guess; a flight number is a fact that dispatch can track in real time. With the flight number on file, your pickup adjusts automatically whether you land 40 minutes early or 90 minutes late — no phone call needed on your end, no fee for the change.

## Book the Return Trip When You Book the Arrival

Frequent flyers rarely leave the departure transfer for later. Booking both legs together locks in your pickup for the return flight, which matters most for early-morning departures — the exact scenario where an unreserved ride is hardest to arrange reliably. It also means one clean pair of confirmations instead of remembering to book a second trip mid-vacation.

## Know Where You're Actually Being Picked Up

MSY's terminal is compact, with baggage claim and ground transportation on the lower level. Standard curbside pickup happens at the designated arrivals curb outside baggage claim; if you've added meet-and-greet, your chauffeur will be waiting inside, at the bottom of the escalators leading down to baggage claim, holding a name sign. Knowing which one you booked before you land avoids the ten minutes of confusion that trips up first-time visitors.

## Build In Extra Time During Festival Season — Even With a Reserved Car

A flat-rate reservation protects your price and your pickup, but it can't erase Mardi Gras parade closures or Jazz Fest traffic. During major event weeks, mention your hotel or destination neighborhood when you book so your chauffeur can plan the route in advance, and build a little extra cushion into your own schedule for anything time-sensitive, like a dinner reservation or a flight connection.

## Keep Your Phone On After Landing

You almost never need it — a well-run transfer requires no action from you — but keeping your phone reachable after touchdown gives dispatch a channel if something unusual happens, like a bag going to the wrong carousel or a gate change that affects which door you exit through.

## Give the Exact Spelling for a Meet-and-Greet Sign

If someone else booked your trip — an assistant, a colleague, a family member — double-check the spelling of the name that will be on the sign. It's a small detail, but a misspelled name undercuts the whole point of a personalized greeting, especially for a client or VIP arrival.

## Note Special Needs at Booking, Not at the Curb

A child car seat installed before you land, a wheelchair-accessible pickup, or extra oversized luggage are all easy to accommodate with advance notice and much harder to improvise on the spot. If you know you'll need something specific, say so when you book rather than mentioning it for the first time at the curb.

## Understand What "Complimentary Wait Time" Actually Covers

Every MSY airport pickup includes 45 minutes of complimentary wait time on domestic arrivals and 60 minutes on international arrivals, timed from actual touchdown — not the scheduled arrival time. That window comfortably covers a slow customs line, a delayed bag, or a coffee stop before you head to the curb. Frequent flyers use this window without a second thought; it's part of what the flat rate already includes.

## For Groups: Consolidate Rather Than Split

Travelers who fly with the same group repeatedly learn quickly that one right-sized vehicle beats several smaller ones — fewer coordination headaches, often a lower per-person cost, and everyone arrives together instead of in staggered waves. ## Cruise Passengers: Book the Transfer With the Flights, Not Separately

If you're flying into MSY before boarding a ship at the Port of New Orleans, book the port transfer at the same time as your flight, not as an afterthought once you're already in town. The port sits about 20 to 30 minutes from the airport, and a reserved transfer removes the single biggest variable on an embarkation morning with a fixed sailing time.

## Learn the Difference Between Curbside and Meet-and-Greet Before You Book

Frequent flyers pick their pickup style deliberately rather than defaulting to whatever's cheapest. Curbside works well for a light traveler who knows the airport and just wants to get moving. Meet-and-greet earns its cost back quickly for anyone managing kids, heavy luggage, or an unfamiliar terminal — the difference of a chauffeur handling the carousel versus doing it yourself is bigger than it sounds after a long flight.

## Ask About Add-Ons Before You Need Them

Meet-and-greet, child seats, and extra stops are all easy to add when you book and can be harder to arrange last-minute, particularly during a busy week. If there's a chance you'll want one, ask about it at booking rather than waiting to see if you need it.

## Know the Cancellation Window

Plans change. Sedan and SUV reservations cancel free of charge up to 3 hours before pickup; Sprinter vans, limousines, and special-event bookings up to 12 hours before. Knowing this window in advance means a schedule change never turns into an unnecessary charge.

## Trust the Flat Rate — Don't Re-Shop It on the Day

One habit frequent travelers drop quickly: checking rideshare prices against their already-confirmed car service on the day of the trip. The flat rate was set for a reason — it holds regardless of what a rideshare app happens to be showing that morning, which is exactly the point of booking it in the first place.

## Terminal Navigation Tips Worth Knowing

MSY's single-terminal layout is genuinely easy to learn, but a few details help on the first pass: baggage claim and all ground transportation sit on the lower level, reached by escalators from the concourse above, and the arrivals curb is a short walk from those escalators. If you're connecting from an international flight, build a little extra time into your own expectations for customs, even though your chauffeur's wait-time window already accounts for it — a relaxed traveler makes better decisions than a rushed one.

## Seasonal Weather Considerations

Louisiana summers bring heat and sudden afternoon thunderstorms that can affect both flight schedules and road conditions on I-10; hurricane season, roughly June through November, occasionally affects travel more significantly. None of this changes how a reserved transfer works — flight tracking and the wait-time window handle routine weather delays automatically — but frequent flyers keep an eye on the forecast before a summer or fall trip and mention any known weather risk to dispatch, especially for a tight connection or an early event booking.

## Reading Your Confirmation Before You Fly

A final habit worth adopting: read your written confirmation once before your trip, not just when it arrives. Confirm the flight number is correct, the destination address is right, and any add-ons you requested — meet-and-greet, a car seat — are actually listed. Catching a typo a day before you fly is a two-minute fix; catching it at the curb is a much bigger hassle.

## Traveling With Connections Through MSY

Most trips through MSY are origin-or-destination flights rather than connections, but for the travelers who do have a tight layover before or after their New Orleans leg, it's worth telling dispatch the full context — not just the New Orleans flight, but the fact that a missed connection elsewhere could shift your actual travel day entirely. This doesn't change how flight tracking works, but it helps dispatch understand the stakes if your schedule needs to flex.

## A Quick Pre-Trip Checklist

- Flight number provided, not just an estimated time.

- Return trip booked alongside the arrival, if applicable.

- Pickup style chosen — curbside or meet-and-greet — and understood.

- Any special requests (car seat, extra stop, specific vehicle) noted at booking.

- Confirmation reviewed for accuracy a day or two before departure.

## Passing These Tips Along

Frequent travelers often become the person a family or office turns to for travel advice, and MSY transfers are a common example — someone who's flown through Louis Armstrong International a dozen times becomes the default source of "how does this work" for a colleague's first visit. These habits are easy to pass along precisely because none of them require special knowledge, just a slightly more deliberate approach to booking than simply picking the first option that comes up. Share the flight-number habit and the return-trip habit first — those two alone prevent the majority of avoidable airport transfer headaches, and they take no extra time to put into practice on the very next trip.
